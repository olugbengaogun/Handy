## What's new in 1.1.0

### Handy Plus now learns from your corrections

Fix a transcript in History and Handy Plus pays attention. When you make the
same correction a second time, it offers to apply it from then on — so the
words it keeps getting wrong get fixed once, not forever.

Nothing is learned from a single edit. A one-off typo, or a moment where you
simply reworded something, should never become a permanent rule, so a
correction has to recur before it is even suggested. Rewrites are watched but
never promoted: changing your mind about phrasing is not a transcription
mistake. And every rule stays visible and removable — a system that learns
from you is only worth trusting if you can see everything it has concluded.

### A new Vocabulary tab

Custom Words and your Personal Dictionary have moved out of Advanced and into
one place, alongside the corrections Handy Plus suggests and the names it has
noticed you use often. Previously the same personal dictionary was presented
as two unrelated features in two different screens.

If you are just getting started, Handy Plus now reads back through your own
past transcripts and offers the recurring names and terms it finds, so the
dictionary is useful before you have corrected anything at all.

### Corrections that actually apply

Teaching Handy Plus a correction previously had no effect unless you had cloud
post-processing switched on — the "heard as" side of every entry in your
personal dictionary was collected and then discarded. It is now applied
directly, on your machine, with no model involved.

Matching is whole-word, so a rule for a short word can never fire inside a
longer one, and a correction is never applied to text you spelled out by hand.

### Quiet recordings

Audio that comes in too quiet is now lifted toward a usable level before
transcription. It only ever boosts — if your microphone is already well set
up, nothing changes.

### Post-processing can no longer run away with your text

When post-processing is enabled, its output is checked against what you
actually said. If a model answers your dictation instead of tidying it, or
summarises it, or otherwise strays too far, the result is thrown away and your
original transcription is kept. Ordinary cleanup — punctuation, capitalisation,
removing filler words — is unaffected.

### Behind the scenes

A weekly check now watches whether the model catalog, the speech engine or the
release cadence has fallen behind the upstream Handy project, so new models
reach you promptly rather than whenever someone remembers to look.

Handy Plus also ships an accuracy benchmark that measures word error rate and,
more usefully, how often a transcript needed any edit at all — against your own
speech, using transcripts you have already corrected as the reference.
