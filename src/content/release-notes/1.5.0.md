## What's new in 1.5.0

This one is mostly catching up with Handy upstream, and the headline is a
sturdier microphone. Everything from 1.4.0 is unchanged.

- **Handy recovers when your microphone disappears.** Unplug a dock, walk away
  from AirPods, or have an interface drop off mid-session, and Handy used to
  keep listening to a device that was no longer there — silently, until you
  restarted it. It now falls back to your default microphone instead. If you
  dictate through anything other than the built-in mic, this is the fix worth
  having.
- **The start sound now means the microphone is genuinely ready.** It used to
  play on a fixed timer after you pressed the key, which was a guess: on a slow
  device you could start talking before anything was being recorded, and lose
  the first word or two. It now waits for real audio to arrive, so when you hear
  it, you can speak. There is a clearer visual cue while the microphone is
  waking up, too.
- **Your appearance setting reaches the whole app.** Choosing Light or Dark
  explicitly used to leave the recording overlay and the window title bar
  following macOS instead of you. Both now follow your choice, along with the
  card and shadow colours.
- **Your Personal Dictionary accepts phrases, not just single words.** Two-word
  names and product terms can now be taught properly.
- **You can finally see the filler-word setting.** Handy Plus has always removed
  "um" and "uh", with no way to turn it off — the switch existed but was not
  shown anywhere. It now appears under Settings → Advanced. It also works out
  which language you actually spoke rather than assuming, so it no longer strips
  words that are only fillers in English. Removing "you know" and "I mean" stays
  a separate setting, unchanged.
- **A menu bar icon glitch is gone.** Starting and stopping quickly could leave
  the tray icon showing the wrong state.
- **Quieter when idle.** Always-on microphone mode does less work between
  dictations.

Under the hood, the speech engine has been updated. You will not notice it
today, but it is what lets newer speech models load at all, so it is the piece
that keeps the model list useful over time. Two new models also appear —
a German-tuned Parakeet and MOSS (English and Chinese) — but neither beats
Parakeet Unified EN for English dictation, so there is no reason to switch.

Transcription accuracy and speed are otherwise unchanged, and everything still
runs on your Mac.
